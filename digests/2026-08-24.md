### 🔴 Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting Requirement Revision (CTA/BOI final rule) — relevance 2 (international, no single core bucket)
**Type:** Final rule (FinCEN) · **Effective:** 2026-08-14 · **Comment deadline:** n/a (final rule)
**Source:** https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision
**Plain English:** FinCEN's final rule (adopting, with limited changes, the March 2025 interim final rule) permanently redefines "reporting company" under the Corporate Transparency Act to mean **only entities formed under foreign law that have registered to do business in a U.S. State or Tribal jurisdiction**. Domestically-formed companies and U.S. persons are entirely out of the CTA/BOI reporting regime going forward — no BOI report, no updates, no company-applicant reporting. Foreign-formed reporting companies still must report BOI, but only for **foreign** beneficial owners; U.S.-person beneficial owners of a foreign reporting company are exempt from being reported. FinCEN will also delete previously-filed U.S.-person BOI data from its database.
**Why you care:** This is a practice-changing compliance development, not a routine notice — I initially mis-scored this item as 0 based on an incomplete abstract read that only surfaced the "U.S. person exemption" half of the holding, missing the "reporting company" redefinition. For a foreign-trust practice: any client structure with a **domestic** entity (LLC, corp) previously subject to BOI reporting is now fully out of scope — that compliance task drops off client checklists entirely. But any **foreign-formed entity registered to do business in a U.S. state** within a client's structure (e.g., a foreign corporation used by a foreign trust) still has a live BOI obligation for its foreign beneficial owners. Worth flagging proactively to clients with either fact pattern.
**Correction note:** This item was originally placed in the Skip list below (still visible in earlier digest history/state notes) based on a narrow abstract extraction; caught and corrected the same day after a manual news check surfaced the full scope of the rule.

### 🟡 Foreign Currency Gain or Loss of Controlled Foreign Corporations — relevance 2 (foreign_corp_partnership, anti_deferral)
**Type:** Proposed Rule (NPRM) · **IRC:** §987, §988 (QBU foreign currency rules), CFC context (§957/§958) · **Comment deadline:** 2026-11-12
**Source:** https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16569/foreign-currency-gain-or-loss-of-controlled-foreign-corporations
**Plain English:** Proposed regs on determining and recognizing foreign currency gain or loss on qualified business units (QBUs) of CFCs. Would add an election letting a CFC skip computing/recognizing FX gain or loss on a remittance from a QBU, except for certain inbound nonrecognition transactions.
**Why you care:** Direct hit on foreign_corp_partnership (CFC) — relevant to any client with a CFC operating through a QBU with a different functional currency. The optional election could simplify (or complicate, depending on facts) FX gain/loss reporting on QBU remittances. Comment window open to 2026-11-12.

### 🟡 Application of Section 250(b)(3)(A)(i)(VII) to Sales or Other Dispositions of Property — relevance 2 (anti_deferral)
**Type:** Proposed Rule (NPRM) · **IRC:** §250 · **Comment deadline:** 2026-10-05
**Source:** https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/20/2026-17019/application-of-section-250b3aivii-to-sales-or-other-dispositions-of-property
**Plain English:** Proposed regs under §250 addressing which income of a domestic corporation is excluded from deduction-eligible income (DEI) — the FDII computation base — specifically around income/gain from sales or other dispositions of property (including intangibles and depreciable assets).
**Why you care:** §250 is a named anti_deferral bucket term (FDII/GILTI deduction mechanics). Relevant to any domestic-corporation client computing FDII where sales of intangible or depreciable property are part of the income mix.
